> In 1935, Antal Németh took over the management of the National Theatre in Budapest and commissioned Ferenc Farkas to write incidental music for William Shakespeare’s play « Timon of Athens ». Recalling the creation of this score, Farkas said:
>
> "I wanted to compose music with Greek sounds. So I experimented with an arrangement without strings, using only wind instruments, as well as saxophone, piano, harp, and percussion. By that time, I had already gained valuable experience composing for film, but I still had to learn how to write more subtle music to accompany the dialogue. In film, the volume is modulated; in the theater, however—as the critics pointed out—my music drowned out Shakespeare’s words. You learn from your mistakes!"
>
> In 1938, Farkas adapted his incidental music into a suite, which he orchestrated in 1967 for wind orchestra, percussion, and piano.
>
>The suite consists of three movements: “Marcia d’Alcibia” (March of Alcibiades), “Asztali zene” (Dinner Music), and “Tánc” (Dance).
> {{< author name="László Gombos" >}}

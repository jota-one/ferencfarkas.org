> Budapest 1950. Musicians were obliged by the new communist regime to adopt a new style of music “easy to understand and in a state of constant optimism”. For fear of sanctions, I stopped using dodecaphony. In order to support my family, I accepted a number of orders. Realism being in fashion, I composed cantatas on popular themes: "On the banks of the Tisza" is one of them.
>
> I composed this cantata in February 1950. It consists of three movements, each built upon variations of the same Hungarian folk song:

{{% /column %}}
{{% column %}}

> 1. “Tisza partján nem jó mélyen aludni” (It is not good to sleep deeply on the banks of the Tisza)
> 2. Ballad “Megöltek egy legényt” (They killed a young man)
> 3. “Mély a Tiszának a széle” (The bank of the Tisza is deep) 
>
> The orchestral accompaniment and the interludes seek to express the dramatic atmosphere of the three songs.
> {{< author name="Ferenc Farkas" >}}

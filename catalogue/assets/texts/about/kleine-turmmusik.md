> In 1967, the small town of Nyírbátor, located in northeastern Hungary, commissioned a work from Ferenc Farkas for the opening of its first "Musical Days," an event that later became a prestigious music festival. 
>
> Nyírbátor owes its fame to the powerful Báthory family who, from the 14th to the 17th century, made this city their political, military and cultural center and who welcomed the famous 16th century Hungarian epic poet, chronicler, composer and minstrel, Sebestyén Tinódi Lantos.
>
> Farkas’s composition for three trumpets, four horns, three trombones, and tuba draws inspiration from Renaissance fanfares. It is divided into four sections: following a majestic introduction, there is an arrangement of a melody by Sebestyén Tinódi Lantos. After a lively passage in triple meter, the motif from the introduction reappears as a finale.

> Guillevic is one of the major French poets of the 20th century and a passionate translator; he translated the works of József Attila - one of the most prominent figures in 20th-century Hungarian literature and poetry - into French.
>
> The first piece in this cycle of three songs for voice and piano, “Le menuisier” (“The Carpenter”), could almost serve as a manifesto for my own work: with what love the carpenter assembles his planks! I, too, assemble words - and, at its core, it is the very same thing. (I have always held the artisan in high regard, just as I place great importance on the technical and craft-based dimensions of art; conversely, I despise negligence, shoddy work, and the sort of disorganized, charlatan-like behavior that cloaks itself in the trappings of genius.)
>
> The second song, “Roses,” is a poem imbued with a delicate eroticism, underscored by dodecaphonic harmonies.
>  
> In its conclusion, the final song, “Le goût de la paix” (“The Taste for Peace”), subtly evokes our willingness to accept anything in order to preserve peace; it is a serene tableau, painted in pastel hues. 
> {{< author name="Ferenc Farkas" >}}

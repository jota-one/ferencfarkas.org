> "Trigón," for flute or clarinet, bassoon, and piano, dates from 1988. The composer writes about his work : "In this case, the title does not simply refer to a triangle, as in trigonometry, but rather to an ancient Greek ball game (trigōnos) played by three people, who are positioned at the corner of a triangle. Like this game, the themes and motifs in my work are passed from one instrument to another."
>
> The first movement, "Moderato molto" begins with a slow, chromatic melody that evokes an imagined music of ancient Greece. This melody recurs twice, punctuating the flow of the music. Perhaps only in the work of Stravinsky can one find a similar blend of such scintillating ideas and density of action. Built from themes that intertwine like vines, the second movement, "Andante moderato," is particularly captivating, as is the lively final "Allegro."
>
> «Trigón», in its version for flute, was premiered in the United States in 1990.
> {{< author name="László Gombos" >}}

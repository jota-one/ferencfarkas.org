> In recent decades, a generation of talented flutists has quietly emerged, who have mastered virtually every technical challenge while maintaining a high level of control and profound musical expression. New works are constantly being created, both for solo wind instruments and for chamber ensembles. 
>
> The flute was an instrument held in particularly high regard by composers of the Baroque and Viennese Classical periods, who delighted both performers and audiences with numerous works written for the solo flute. The Romantic era, while frequently evoking the flute in poetry, largely neglected it in music. For my own part, aside from a few transcriptions, I had taken little interest in the flute until the French flutist Alain Marion, encouraged me to transcribe for him one of my violin sonatas (« Sonatina » for flute and piano). This prompted me to study the instrument more deeply. I decided to compose a concerto for flute and strings. In my view, pitting a full orchestra against a flute would result in an unequal contest, in which the flute’s timbre would be overwhelmed by the power of a large orchestral ensemble; that is why I opted for a string ensemble.

> The "Serenata concertante," composed in 1967, features in its first movement the tripartite structure 
of sonata form: a main theme, a secondary theme, and a final theme. However, the exposition is not 
followed by the traditional development section, but rather by a second exposition in which the 
secondary theme appears in reverse.
> 
> The main theme of the second movement, highly lyrical in character, is followed by a transitional 
section that culminates in a cadenza. This leads into the secondary theme, presented with great 
gentleness in which the flute rises above the undulating accompaniment of muted strings. The main 
theme then reappears in a different key, the transitional section is significantly shortened, and the 
secondary theme is presented in a new form.
>
> The third movement is a rondo in which the theme appears in various forms, yet the characteristic 
rhythm of the « tarantella » remains consistently recognizable. A minor theme in five-beat meter 
precedes a more tranquil, characteristic trio, which alternates cadentially between the main theme 
and its variations.
> {{< author name="Ferenc Farkas" >}}
